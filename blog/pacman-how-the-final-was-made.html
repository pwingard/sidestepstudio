Processing log · September 2026

The Pacman Nebula (NGC 281), HOO

How the final was made

Target: NGC 281 (Sh2-184) with the open cluster IC 1590, in Cassiopeia. Solved centre 00h52m40s +56°39′37″, 2.463″/px.

Rig: Celestron Origin, RASA 6″ 335 mm f/2.2, Origin678C colour camera (IMX678, 2 µm, GBRG), Antlia Triband RGB Ultra (~25 nm bands: red holds Hα+NII+SII, green/blue hold OIII). ISO 1000, uncooled, 17.7–22.5 °C.

Data: 6.5 h in one night, 25–26 September 2026, six Origin sessions — 1,040 × 15 s (4.33 h) + 391 × 20 s (2.17 h), all kept.

Where it was shot: my backyard site at The Star Farm in Camden, South Carolina — Bortle 4.5.

Why HOO, not SHO

SII (672 nm) and Hα (656 nm) land in the same red band on the same red pixels, so they cannot be separated. HOO is the most this filter can honestly give.

Stacking in Siril

  1. Darks matched per session by exposure + ISO, then closest temperature, from a fresh set: dark0019/0022 (15 s, 19.1/20.8 °C) and dark0020/0021 (20 s, 19.8/20.3 °C). The Origin's own stacks were ignored — it calibrated with June 34 °C darks, and a 30 s dark for the 20 s subs.
  2. On this camera the 15–20 s master darks sit below the bias (4872 vs 5000 ADU), so there is no dark current to scale: temperature mismatch handled with a location-based bad-pixel map (find_hot on the dark), not -opt.
  3. Calibrate on the raw CFA, then seqextract_HaOIII before any debayer: red pixels → Hα, green+blue → OIII. Output is half-size, 1928×1090 — each pixel is a 2×2 block, 4.0 µm effective. That is the true Hα resolution of a Bayer camera; a debayer only interpolates the rest.
  4. Each session stacked per channel (winsorized 3/3, additive+scale).

Merging and cleaning the masters

  1. Six sessions merged with Siril star registration — all 12 masters in one sequence on one reference, so Hα and OIII come out already aligned. (combine_nights.py was tried first and blurred stars 4.25 → 5.37 px: it aligns in 8 px steps. Retired for merges; my rule since: alignment is always Siril.) Result: stars 4.6 px, noise ÷2.2 Hα, ÷3 OIII vs the best session.
  2. Row + column banding removed on the linear masters — tools/deband_rowcol.py. The Origin barely dithered (~4 px between subs), so fixed-pattern noise didn't average out. Columns/rows 11%/10% → 7%/3% of pixel noise. (deband.py made columns worse here, 11% → 20%.)
  3. Pre-cropped to full coverage of all six sessions, 1928×1090 → 1887×983 (L 28 · T 95 · R 13 · B 12). The uncropped top band, covered by only some sessions, is what gave an earlier version its top-green / bottom-red sky.

PixInsight — linear

  1. ChannelCombination R = Hα, G = B = OIII; ImageSolver at 4.0 µm / 335 mm.
  2. SPFC narrowband mode, psfMinSNR 40.
  3. MGC with MARS (DR1 + DR2): gradient scale 512, structure separation 5, smoothness 10. HOO works with MARS; SHO would not — MARS has no SII.
  4. SPCC narrowband 656.3 / 500.7 / 500.7 nm at 25 nm bandwidth.
  5. BlurX correct-only, then a stars-only pass at 0.50. MLDenoise 0.90.
  6. StarX (linear, unscreen off).
  7. OIII lift while linear: PixelMath R $T, G/B ($T-med($T))*1.3+med($T) — the med() form lifts the nebula without tinting the sky. (A plain $T*1.3 turned the sky teal; a post-stretch highlight curve flattened the core.)

PixInsight — non-linear

  1. GHS stretch factor 6.27, local intensity 0.05, SP 0.000722, highlight protection 1.0; then black point 0.038.
  2. Curves on the starless; BlurX non-stellar 0.78.
  3. Second StarX on the stretched image, curves lifting the faint outer Hα, stars screened back: ~(~F6*~(F6_stars*1)).

Result

Sky near neutral (median 17/12/12), faint outer Hα 47/31/31, core 236/231/231, 0.04% clipped. Signed bottom-right with “(Origin)”.

Known and accepted: a faint top-teal / bottom-red sky tint (BackgroundNeutralization can't fix a gradient — it made the sky redder in one try); core white rather than teal, because ×1.3 only brings OIII level with Hα there.
