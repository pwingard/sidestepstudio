The Cosmic Question Mark

NGC 7822 (Sh2-171), in Cepheus  ·  SHO with RGB stars — about 33 hours over six nights, September–October 2026

NGC 7822 (Sh2-171)  ·  SHO  ·  ©2026 Peter F. Wingard  ·  @see_theShow

Rims, pillars and dark globules

The Cosmic Question Mark: NGC 7822 (Sh2-171) in Cepheus, about 2,900 light-years away. A star-forming region at the edge of a giant molecular cloud, where hot young stars light up the gas and carve it into rims, pillars and dark globules.

Shown in the SHO palette: sulphur as red, hydrogen as green, oxygen as blue. The blue heart is oxygen lit by the hottest stars, the gold rim is where hydrogen and sulphur take over, and the small dark-green knots are cold globules where only hydrogen shows through. Star colour is real, from separate RGB frames.

Acquisition

Telescope
Askar 80 with 0.8× reducer — 386 mm, f/4.8
Camera
ZWO ASI2600MM Pro
Filters
Chroma 3 nm Ha / OIII / SII, Antlia V-Pro RGB
Integration
Ha 150 × 180s, OIII 274 × 180s, SII 222 × 180s, R / G / B 16 × 60s each, plus 1-second star-core sets = about 33 hours — every sub reviewed before stacking
Dates
September 30 – October 26, 2026 (six nights)
Location
The Starfarm in Camden, South Carolina — Bortle 4
Processing
Stacked in Siril, processed in PixInsight
