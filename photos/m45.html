The Seven Sisters in Their Wedding Dresses

M45, the Pleiades, in Taurus  ·  LRGB — about 9.9 hours over four nights, October 2026

M45, the Pleiades  ·  LRGB  ·  ©2026 Peter F. Wingard  ·  @see_theShow

Veils of blue light

The Seven Sisters in Their Wedding Dresses: M45, the Pleiades, in Taurus. A young cluster of hot blue stars about 444 light-years away and only about 100 million years old, drifting through an unrelated cloud of interstellar dust. The dust doesn’t glow on its own; it scatters the starlight, and blue scatters best, so the sisters wear veils of blue light.

Look closely and the veils are full of fine streaks and filaments, combed out by the stars’ light and the cluster’s motion through the cloud. The brightest stars were kept from burning out by blending in 0.1-second exposures for their cores.

Acquisition

Telescope
Askar 80 with flattener — 495 mm, f/6.2
Camera
ZWO ASI2600MM Pro
Filters
Antlia L / R / G / B
Integration
L 299 × 90s, R 51 × 60s, G 51 × 60s, B 41 × 60s, plus short star-core sets = about 9.9 hours — every sub reviewed before stacking
Dates
October 11–21, 2026 (four nights)
Location
The Starfarm in Camden, South Carolina — Bortle 4
Processing
Stacked in Siril, processed in PixInsight
